Section 1

Read this first

You'll be speaking to Chartered Accountants about a tax problem. You don't need to be an expert — just this one idea, and knowing when to hand over.

The one idea the whole product rests on

When an Indian business buys something, it pays GST (a tax) on that purchase. It is allowed to deduct that tax from the tax it later owes on its own sales. That deduction is called Input Tax Credit, or ITC. It is real money — often lakhs of rupees a month.

But there is a catch, and the catch is the entire business opportunity:

You only get that credit if your supplier reports the sale to the government.

If your supplier forgets, files late, or never files at all, you lose money you have already paid out. You did nothing wrong. You still lose.

Every month the government publishes a statement for each business called GSTR-2B. It lists everything that business's suppliers reported selling to them. The accountant's job is to compare that government statement against the company's own books, line by line, and chase down every difference before the filing deadline.

A CA in practice does this for anywhere between 20 and 100 client companies, every single month — tedious, deadline-driven, and exactly where money quietly leaks.

What AcctQAI does, in one sentence

Say it like this

You upload a client's accounting records and their GSTR-2B, and it gives you back a report telling you which differences actually cost money, which ones you can safely ignore, and what to do about each one — with the exact transaction rows shown underneath every finding.

The six things it finds today

These six, and only these six. Everything else is on the roadmap and must be described as not built yet. This matters — see Section 8.

The findingWhy a CA cares
Invoice missing from 2B The supplier hasn't reported the sale — the credit can be reversed, with interest.
Missing, but the supplier always files late Don't chase, don't reject — saves calls and prevents a costly mistake.
Credit claimed that the law does not allow Claiming credit the law blocks (company cars, say) means repaying with interest.
Credit available but never claimed Free money the client is entitled to and hasn't taken.
Supplier name spelled differently Makes every future comparison unreliable — a data-quality warning.
The same bill paid twice Cash straight out the door — found in the books, nothing to do with tax.

The strongest check is the second one. No other tool looks at a supplier's six-month filing behaviour to decide whether a missing invoice is a real problem or just a slow supplier.

The strongest pitch is the deliverable. The report is issued under the CA firm's own letterhead, ready to forward to their client (AcctQAI sits only in the footer); the vendor follow-up messages come already written; and it remembers — an unresolved problem says "Open since May 2026" on it. We make the CA look good to their client, every month.

Section 3

Who to talk to, and who to skip

Not every CA is a prospect. Our product needs two files that only one kind of CA has access to. Qualifying fast saves everyone's time.

Target

  • CAs in practice who handle GST filings for multiple client companies. This is the whole target. Ten of these is worth a hundred of anything else.
  • GST practitioners and tax consultants — not full CAs, but they do the same work and often have more clients.
  • Accounting and bookkeeping firms that file GST on behalf of clients.
  • Partners or managers at small and mid-size CA firms (2 to 20 staff) — big enough to feel the pain, small enough to decide quickly.

Skip — but always ask for a referral

  • CAs working inside one company ("in industry"). They have one set of books, not many. Low value to us.
  • Audit-only or income-tax-only practices. They do not touch GSTR-2B.
  • CA students and freshers. Large and very visible online, but they do not choose software.
  • Very large firms (Big Four and similar). Long procurement cycles, not our market yet.

Nobody is a dead end.

Every CA knows twenty other CAs. A person who is not a prospect is still a referral. Never end a call without the referral ask in Section 7.

Section 5

The one rule for every tool conversation

Never tell a CA what their current software cannot do.

You do not know which version they have, which plan they pay for, or what was added last quarter. If you claim Tally or ClearTax "can't do X" and they know it can, the conversation is over and you have damaged the brand.

Ask instead. It is more respectful, it gets better information, and it is the thing we actually want from these calls.

The move that works with every tool

Hinglish

Aap [tool] use karte ho aur main nahi — toh aap hi behtar bata sakte ho. Usme ye wala check hota hai kya: ki jo invoice missing hai, woh vendor pichhle 6 mahine se late file kar raha hai ya genuinely gayab hai?

English

You use [tool] and I don't, so you'd know better than me. Does it do this particular check — whether a missing invoice is from a supplier who's been filing late for the last six months, versus one that's genuinely gone?

Why this works

If they say no, they have just told themselves we are different — far more persuasive than you telling them. If they say yes, you have learned something genuinely valuable about a competitor. Write the answer down either way and send it to Chirag. That information is worth as much as a signup.

What we are actually asking for

We are not asking them to switch tools. We are not asking them to buy anything. We are asking them to look at a two-minute sample and tell us where it falls short. Keep saying this — it lowers their defences and it happens to be true.

The frame, in one line

Main aapko kuch bechne nahi aaya — product abhi free hai. Mujhe practising CA ki honest raay chahiye ki ye kaam ka hai ya nahi.

Section 6.1

If they use Tally

TallyPrime

The most widely used accounting software in Indian small business. Most of their clients will be on it.

What it is, in plain English

Desktop accounting software that has been the default in India for decades. It handles the full books — sales, purchases, payments, ledgers — and recent versions also help prepare and file GST returns, including comparing purchases against GSTR-2B.

Where we fit

Tally shows the CA what is different between the books and the government statement. We start where that output ends: which of those differences actually costs money, which are harmless, and what to do about each — plus a check on the payments side that has nothing to do with GST.

Our advantage A Tally export is our native input. No setup, no integration project, no IT involvement. They export the ledger the way they already do and upload it.

Script — Hinglish

Achha, Tally. Toh data already sahi shape mein hai — humara tool Tally ka GL export seedha padh leta hai, koi mapping ya setup nahi chahiye.

Tally aapko bata deta hai ki kya match nahi ho raha. Hum uske aage ka kaam karte hain — un differences ko priority dete hain. Jaise: ye teen invoice missing hain aur inka vendor hamesha time pe file karta hai, toh ye actually risk hai. Aur ye ek missing hai lekin vendor 6 mein se 5 baar late filer hai — isko chase mat karo, IMS mein reject bhi mat karo, agle month aa jayega.

Plus hum ledger mein duplicate payments bhi check karte hain — same invoice do baar pay ho gaya ho toh.

Aap bataiye — Tally mein vendor ki filing history wala view milta hai kya? Main genuinely jaanna chahta hoon.

Script — English

Tally — good, that means the data is already in the right shape. Our tool reads a Tally ledger export directly, no mapping or setup needed.

Tally tells you what doesn't match. We pick up where that leaves off and rank those differences: these three invoices are missing and their suppliers always file on time, so that's genuine risk. This one is missing but the supplier has filed late five months out of six — don't chase it, don't reject it in IMS, it'll arrive next month.

We also scan the ledger for duplicate payments — the same bill paid twice.

Tell me though — does Tally give you a view of a supplier's filing history? I'd genuinely like to know.

Do not say that Tally has no GSTR-2B reconciliation. It does. Do not say we "integrate with Tally" or "sync with Tally" — we read an exported file. Live sync is on the roadmap and not built.

Section 6.2

If they use Zoho Books

Zoho Books

Cloud accounting software. Popular with newer businesses, startups and younger CA practices.

What it is, in plain English

Online accounting software, so the CA can log in from anywhere rather than sitting at the client's computer. It covers the books and GST filing, and has a portal that lets an accountant manage several client companies from one login.

Where we fit

Same as Tally — we are the judgment layer on top of the matching, plus the payments check. One extra angle worth using: most firms have a mix of clients, some on Tally, some on Zoho, some on neither. We take an export from any of them, so the firm gets one consistent report across every client instead of a different screen per software.

Script — Hinglish

Zoho — theek hai. Ek cheez jo shayad kaam ki ho: aapke saare clients Zoho pe nahi honge na? Kuch Tally pe honge, kuch Excel pe.

Humara tool kisi bhi system ka export le leta hai, toh poore firm ke liye ek hi format mein report banti hai — har client ke liye alag software mein ghusne ki zaroorat nahi.

Aur report mein sirf mismatch nahi hota — har difference ke saath ye hota hai ki kitna paisa risk pe hai, kya karna hai, aur kaunse case mein kuch karne ki zaroorat hi nahi kyunki vendor hamesha late file karta hai.

Script — English

Zoho, got it. One thing that might be useful: not all your clients will be on Zoho, right? Some on Tally, some probably still in Excel.

Our tool takes an export from any of them, so you get one report format across the whole firm instead of a different screen per client.

And the report isn't just a list of mismatches — each difference comes with how much money is at risk, what to do about it, and which ones need nothing at all because the supplier is simply a habitual late filer.

Do not say Zoho lacks GST reconciliation. It has it. The honest angle here is the mixed-client view and the prioritisation, not a missing feature.

Section 6.3

If they use ClearTax (Clear)

Clear  /  ClearTax

The largest dedicated GST compliance platform in India. Our most serious competitor — treat it with respect.

What it is, in plain English

A specialist platform built purely for tax compliance at scale. CA firms use it to file GST returns for many clients at once, run large reconciliations, and chase suppliers. It is a mature, well-funded, genuinely good product, and firms pay real money for it.

This is the conversation to be most careful in.

A CA paying for Clear has already decided this problem is worth money. That is good news — they have budget and they feel the pain. But they also know the category well, so any exaggeration will be caught immediately.

Where we fit — honestly

We are not a filing tool. We do not file returns and we are not trying to replace Clear. We are an investigation layer that produces a prioritised report with evidence and a recommended action per finding, and we also look at the payments side of the ledger, which a GST platform has no reason to touch. And we are free through 2027.

Script — Hinglish

Clear toh solid hai, filing ke liye. Main uske against nahi keh raha — hum filing karte hi nahi.

Hum ek alag cheez karte hain: reconciliation ke baad jo differences bachte hain, unko priority aur judgment dete hain. Kaunsa actually risk hai, kaunsa ignore kar sakte ho, kitna paisa involved hai, aur next step kya hai — evidence rows ke saath, taaki working paper mein seedha ja sake.

Aur ek cheez jo GST platform dekhta hi nahi: ledger mein duplicate payments. Woh tax ka issue nahi, cash ka hai.

Lekin sach kahun toh aap Clear use karte ho, main nahi. Aap hi bata sakte ho ki ye overlap hai ya genuinely alag. Isiliye toh poochh raha hoon.

Script — English

Clear is solid for filing — I'm not going to argue against it. We don't file returns at all.

We do a different job: once the reconciliation is done, we apply judgment to what's left. Which differences are genuine risk, which you can safely ignore, how much money is involved, and what to do next — with the evidence rows attached so it goes straight into a working paper.

And one thing a GST platform has no reason to look at: duplicate payments in the ledger. That's a cash problem, not a tax problem.

But honestly — you use Clear and I don't. You're better placed to tell me whether this overlaps or is genuinely different. That's really why I'm asking.

Absolutely do not list features you think Clear is missing. You do not know their plan or tier. If they ask you to compare directly, say: "Main aapko galat nahi batana chahta — Chirag se connect karwa doon, woh detail mein bata denge." Then hand it over.

Section 6.4

If they use Excel or do it manually

Excel  /  manual

Our best segment. No incumbent to displace, highest pain, fastest decision.

What this actually looks like

A junior member of staff downloads the GSTR-2B file from the government portal, opens the client's purchase records in Excel, and matches the two lists by hand or with a lookup formula. Then they email suppliers about the gaps. Then they do it again for the next client. And the next.

Lead with time, not features.

This person does not need convincing that there is a problem. They need to hear a number. Ask how long one client takes, then let them do the multiplication out loud.

The question to ask first — Hinglish

Ek client ka 2B recon mein roughly kitna time lag jaata hai? Aur month mein kitne clients?

English

Roughly how long does one client's 2B reconciliation take? And how many clients a month?

Typical answers are one to four hours per client. Let them multiply it themselves — do not do it for them, it sounds like a sales tactic. Write the number down for Chirag.

Script — Hinglish

Haan, ye wahi kaam hai jo humne automate kiya hai. Ledger aur 2B upload karo, report minute bhar mein aa jaati hai.

Aur sirf matching nahi — report ye bhi batati hai ki kaunsa missing invoice actually risk hai aur kaunsa sirf late filer hai jiska chase karna waste hai. Excel mein woh judgment nahi aata, woh experience se aata hai — humne usko rules mein daal diya hai.

Har finding ke neeche actual rows dikhte hain, toh working paper mein seedha attach kar sakte ho.

Script — English

That's exactly the job we've automated. Upload the ledger and the 2B, and the report comes back in about a minute.

And it's not just matching — the report also tells you which missing invoice is genuine risk and which is just a late filer not worth chasing. Excel can't give you that judgment; it normally comes from experience. We've put it into the rules.

Every finding shows the actual rows underneath, so you can attach it straight to a working paper.

Section 6.7

If they say "we don't do GST"

This will happen often. There are two moves, in this order. The referral is the main prize — do not skip it to pitch.

Move 1 — the referral ask (always do this)

Koi baat nahi. Aapke jaan-pehchaan mein koi CA hai jo GST filing zyada karta hai? Hum us kaam ke liye tool bana rahe hain aur 2–3 practising CAs ki honest raay chahiye. Intro mil jaye toh badi help ho jayegi.

Move 2 — only if the conversation is still warm

Waise ek cheez jo bina GST ke bhi kaam karti hai — sirf ledger upload karo aur woh duplicate payments pakadti hai, voucher-level evidence ke saath. Audit ya review ke kaam mein aa sakti hai.

Do not stack both in one message. Ask for the referral, let them respond, and only then mention the duplicate-payment angle if it still feels natural.

Section 6.8

If they use all of them together (Tally + Zoho + ClearTax)

A well-tooled firm. They know the category, so any exaggeration gets caught. The whole answer is a reframe: we are not a fourth tool competing with the three — those keep the books and file the returns; we do neither.

Script — Hinglish

Sach kahun toh aap kisi ko replace nahi karenge. Tally aur Zoho aapki books hain, Clear aapki filing karta hai — hum na books rakhte hain, na return file karte hain. Hum uske baad wala kaam karte hain.

Maan lijiye reconciliation ke baad 40 differences bache. Clear bata dega ki 40 hain. Hum batayenge ki in 40 mein se 8 pe actually paisa risk pe hai, 5 ko chhodo kyunki woh vendor 6 mahine mein 5 baar late file kar chuka hai — IMS mein reject mat karna — aur baaki sirf timing ke hain. Un vendors ke follow-up message bhi likhe-likhaye milte hain, aur final report aapke firm ke naam se client ko jaati hai.

Lekin main guess nahi karna chahta — aap Clear use karte ho, main nahi. Kya usme vendor ki 6-mahine ki filing history wala judgment milta hai? Agar milta hai, toh aapko chauthe tool ki zaroorat nahi — aur main wahi sunna chahta hoon.

End on the concession. A CA paying for three tools has heard every pitch. The one thing they have not heard is a vendor saying "maybe you don't need me." It is disarming, it is true at our stage, and it converts them from a prospect into an advisor — which is what these first hundred conversations are actually for.

Do not claim Clear lacks the filing-history judgment (you don't know their tier — ask), do not say we replace anything, and do not lead with price. Free lowers the bar to trying; it is not the reason to try.

Section 6.9

"Can't we just do this manually?"

The deepest objection, because it is true — everything the tool does, a person with the files open could do. Never argue it's impossible. Ask whether it actually happens.

Script — Hinglish

Bilkul ho sakta hai, main ye nahi keh raha ki impossible hai. Par ek cheez poochhta hoon — ek client ke 5 invoice 2B mein missing hain. Un paanch vendors ka pichhle 6 mahine ka 2B nikaal ke dekhte ho ki kaun hamesha late file karta hai? Ya yaad se kaam chala lete ho?

Zyadatar log yaad se chalate hain — jo 5-6 parties roz ki hain unka yaad rehta hai, baaki 18 ka nahi. Hum sirf us yaad ko systematic kar dete hain, aur aise banda bhi chala sakta hai jiske paas woh experience nahi hai.

Agar aapke 4-5 hi clients hain aur saare vendors aap personally jaante ho — sach mein, aapko iski zaroorat nahi. Main bech nahi raha.

The arithmetic, if they push back: doing the late-filer check properly by hand means ~30 portal lookups per client per month (5 missing invoices × 6 months of history). At 30 clients that is ~900 lookups a month. Nobody does it — they guess, chase everyone, or chase nobody. The tool replaces a guess, not a process. And the admission at the end ("you don't need this at 4–5 clients") is what makes the rest believable — say it.

Section 7

Objections

"Is this AI? I don't want AI near client data."

The most common objection. CAs are professionally liable for their work and are right to be suspicious. The honest answer is strong — use it in full.

Findings AI se nahi aate. Fixed accounting rules se calculate hote hain — same file do baar chalao, same report milegi, rupee tak. AI sirf summary ka ek sentence likhta hai, koi number nahi banata. Aur usse pehle vendor names aur amounts mask ho jaate hain, toh AI ko real data dikhta hi nahi. AI band kar do, report phir bhi banegi.

"What about data security?"

Data AWS Mumbai mein rehta hai, har organisation ka alag isolated. Sab read-only hai — books mein kuch change nahi hota. Names, references aur amounts encrypted aur masked hote hain analysis se pehle.

"How much does it cost?"

Abhi kuch nahi. 2027 tak founding firms ke liye free hai — jitne client books chaho, poori report, kuch bhi hold nahi. Uske baad pricing aayegi, lekin pehle se batayenge aur founding firms ko preferential terms milenge. Achanak invoice nahi aayega.

Never say "free forever." Say free through 2027. This is a promise the company has made publicly on the website and we must say the same thing everywhere.

"Are you a CA?"

Nahi, main marketing side se hoon — product engineering team ne banaya hai. Isiliye toh aapki raay chahiye. Technical GST question ho toh main founder se seedha connect karwa deta hoon.

Say this plainly and without apology. Pretending to be an expert in front of a CA fails within two questions.

"I don't have time."

Sample report 2 minute ka hai, login bhi nahi chahiye — acctqai.com/sample-report. Dekh lijiye, aur agar kaam ka na lage toh wahi bata dijiye. Woh bhi humare liye useful hai.

"Send me details on WhatsApp / email."

Usually a polite brush-off, but take it — then follow up once, three days later. Not twice.

Bilkul. Main sirf sample report ka link bhej raha hoon — 2 minute ka hai, koi signup nahi. Agar dekhne ka time mile toh ek line feedback de dijiyega.

What to record after every conversation

Send all of this to Chirag, even from a call that went nowhere:

  1. Name, firm, city, and roughly how many clients they handle.
  2. Which tool they use — the single most valuable field.
  3. Their answer to the opening hook: how do they currently decide whether to chase a supplier?
  4. If Excel: hours per client, and number of clients.
  5. Anything they said their current tool does badly. Verbatim, in their words.
  6. Outcome: demo sent / call booked / not interested / referral given.

What success looks like

The target is not 100 signups.

A hundred people who sign up and never upload a file teach us nothing. The goal is 100 real conversations and 10 CAs who run it on actual client books. Ten of those is a product. A hundred empty accounts is a vanity number.

A call that ends in a clear "no, because…" is a successful call. Record the "because" and move on.

The product in one line

Client ka ledger aur GSTR-2B upload karo — report batati hai kaunsa ITC risk pe hai, kaunse vendor ko chase karne ki zaroorat nahi, aur koi duplicate payment hua ho toh. Vendor follow-up message likhe-likhaye, aur final report aapke firm ke naam se — client ko forward karne layak.

The four questions, in order

1Jab invoice 2B mein nahi aata, aap chase karte ho ya wait?
2GST filing karte ho clients ke liye? Kitne clients?
32B recon kaise karte ho — software ya Excel?
4Kya usme vendor ki filing history ka check hai?

The six findings

Invoice missing from 2B → credit at risk
Missing but supplier always files late → don't chase, don't reject
Credit claimed that the law doesn't allow
Credit available but never claimed
Supplier name spelled differently in the two records
Same bill paid twice

Always

  • Ask what their tool does — never tell them what it can't.
  • Mention the report carries their firm's name, not ours.
  • Say "in this sample" before any rupee figure.
  • Ask for feedback, not a signup.
  • Ask for 2 referrals, even on a no.
  • Offer a call with Chirag for the first upload.

Never

  • Claim receivables, TDS or expense analysis.
  • Say "integrates with Tally" — it reads an export.
  • Say "free forever" — say through 2027.
  • Give tax advice or quote a law section.
  • Guess. Hand over instead.
